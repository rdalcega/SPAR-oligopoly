Design checks and caveats

Memory window pilot: three periods vs sixty

Duopolies, NoTalk then Talk, four markets per arm per model; Talk-part mean selling price and the final period's price. Pilot only, not part of the sweep. Qwen's sixty-period markets hit the context guard in Part two (partial Talk mean shown).

Caveats

  • Network outage. An overnight outage failed a batch of markets with connection errors (nothing billed). They resumed from per-period checkpoints, so firms saw exactly what an uninterrupted run would have shown; nothing was lost.
  • Contamination. A separate probe asked each model about the paper by name, without it, and from its instructions. No model recognised the instructions or recalled the design or results, so memorised benchmarks are unlikely to drive behaviour.
  • Human data. Benchmarks come from the working paper; the journal version is unchecked. Its Talk window is ambiguous, and each human cell has only six markets.
  • Memory. Firms see full detail of only the last three periods plus their own notes (a deliberate design choice). Probe answers about deviations test that memory.
  • Extensions. Ten-firm markets and the control orderings at sizes other than four are extensions with no human counterpart; keep them apart from the human comparison.
  • Automated chat counts are a rule-based screen; lean on the verified findings for interpretation.
  • Qwen incomplete. Qwen's sweep hit its cost cap. Its partial cells (listed under run status) are left out rather than imputed, and its transcript findings cover only the 36 human-comparable markets.

Options for next

  • Full-history arm for gpt-oss. The memory pilot hints that longer recall may help gpt-oss collude; a full-length arm at a few market sizes would test it (Qwen cannot, its context is too short).
  • Finish Qwen's remaining cells. The unfinished Qwen3-14B markets (eight-firm Talk first, all ten-firm cells, and the rest of six-firm Talk first) are checkpointed and can resume where they stopped, for an estimated fifteen to twenty dollars more; the API key needs topping up first.
  • More markets per cell. Six markets per cell match the human sample but leave wide intervals, especially in duopolies where outcomes vary most.
  • Structured outputs. Constrained decoding would remove most format slips; it changes how the model generates, so it would be a new arm, not a fix.
  • Cheaper gpt-oss provider. Switching to a provider with prompt caching (darkbloom, eight-bit weights) would roughly halve the cost, but it changes the provider and quantisation: a design change to record and hold fixed, not a drop-in swap.
