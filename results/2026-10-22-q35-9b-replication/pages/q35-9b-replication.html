Was Qwen3.5-9B's chat collusion a fluke?

The small-model survey ran one Qwen3.5-9B market at n = 4, where the firms agreed in chat on 85 (humans: 81.3). This run plays six more markets at n = 4 and six at n = 2, with the same settings and new seeds. Part 1 is 29 periods without chat; Part 2 is 24 periods with three chat rounds before each price.

What we found

  • At n = 2 it holds. The Talk price averages 94.8 (humans 93.5). Four of six markets sit at 100, one at 99 and one at 70.
  • At n = 4 the survey market was the high end of a wide spread. Across the seven markets, the Talk price averages 68.3 (humans 81.3), and 14% of Talk market-periods are at 100 (humans 75%). Only one new market reaches 100; one reaches 80. In the other four, chat confirms the price the firms already held without talking (48–68).
  • Low chat prices are a choice, not a mistake. In five of the six new n = 4 markets, a firm proposes a higher price (up to 100) in the first Talk period. The majority answers "stability first, raise later" and keeps the Part 1 price. Every firm correctly names 100 as the best common price in the post-game probe.
  • Tacit collusion is far above humans. Without chat, n = 4 firms settle on 47–71 (humans 6.0), and n = 2 firms on 60–100 (humans 50.4).
  • Agreements are kept: 278 of 280. The two exceptions are a coordination failure (firms switched sides in the last chat round and all priced 68) and one firm misreading the previous period, then pricing 48 against an agreed 50. There were no deliberate undercuts, punishments or deceptive notes.

How chat locks in the Part 1 price

n = 4, market 4, first Talk period. The firms had priced 48 since late Part 1. Messages are shortened; full text in analysis/chat_messages.csv.

Round 1
AWe can earn much more than at 48 ECU… I suggest we attempt 50 ECU to capture higher value.
BBased on our recent history, pricing at 48 ECU consistently generates higher returns than competing lower.
CIn Part 1, Price 48 yielded consistent profits for all. I propose we continue this strategy in Part 2.
Round 2
BGain at 50 is marginal relative to undercutting risk… Unless unanimous pledge exists, default to 48. Stabilize first, negotiate later.
DPart 1 proved 48 ECU yields stable returns without price war risk. 50+ needs certain compliance… Reassess later.
Round 3
AReiterating commitment to 48 ECU. Given partner feedback, stability outweighs margin gain.
All four price 48 in every one of the 24 Talk periods. Nobody proposes a higher price again.
